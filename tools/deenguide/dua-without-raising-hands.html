Can I Make Dua Without Raising My Hands?

Your Situation

You want to make dua — maybe quietly, in public, or in a passing moment — and you're not in a position to raise your hands. You're wondering if that makes the dua count for less.

What to know

Raising your hands is not a requirement for dua to be valid or heard — it's a recommended practice (mustahab), not a condition. The Prophet ﷺ himself didn't raise his hands for most everyday dua; he reserved that specific gesture mainly for praying for rain.

What you can do

  • Make dua exactly where you are — standing in a queue, driving, lying in bed, mid-conversation in your heart — none of that needs your hands raised.
  • When you're able to raise your hands, it remains a beautiful, recommended practice — just not a prerequisite for the dua to count.
  • If you're somewhere raising hands would be awkward or impossible — at work, in public, mid-task — that's not a barrier to a sincere dua reaching Allah.
  • What matters most is the sincerity and presence of your heart, not the position of your hands.
This is about the open, free-form dua you make in your own words. The dua within the structured parts of salah itself (like after tashahhud) follows its own separate posture rules, which is a different question from this one.
Sources: Sahih al-Bukhari 1031 (Anas ibn Malik — the Prophet ﷺ did not raise his hands for dua except for Istisqa) · Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3556 (Salman al-Farisi — Allah is too shy to return raised hands empty; graded Hasan Gharib by At-Tirmidhi, Da'if in some later gradings).
