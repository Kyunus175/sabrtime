I Feel Guilty Prioritizing Prayer Over Family Time

Your Situation

You're trying to be consistent with prayer, Quran, or extra worship, but it's starting to feel like it's coming at the cost of time with your spouse, kids, or family — and the guilt cuts both ways.

What to know

This exact tension came up between two companions, Salman al-Farisi and Abu Darda, and the Prophet ﷺ settled it directly: worship, self, and family all carry real rights over you, and none of them is meant to swallow the others.

What you can do

  • Recall the hadith itself: when Salman saw Abu Darda neglecting his family for extra night prayers, he told him plainly, "Your Lord has a right over you, your own self has a right over you, and your family has a right over you — give each its due." The Prophet ﷺ confirmed: "Salman has spoken the truth." (Sahih al-Bukhari 6139)
  • Notice what this hadith is not saying: it's not telling Abu Darda to abandon night prayer, only to stop treating it as the only right that matters.
  • In practice: protect the five obligatory prayers as non-negotiable, but let voluntary extras — long Quran sessions, extra nawafil — flex around real family needs rather than the other way around.
  • A short, focused prayer done on time with a present heart, followed by real attention to your family, often reflects this balance better than a long prayer that leaves you resentful or distracted afterward.
  • If it's your spouse or kids who feel neglected specifically, ask them directly what would actually help — sometimes it's less total time and more a specific, predictable slot they can count on.
This is about balance, not an excuse to cut back on the five daily prayers themselves — those aren't in the "flexible" category this hadith describes. What's flexible is everything beyond the obligation: how long you sit afterward, how many voluntary prayers you add, how you spend time that isn't otherwise claimed.
Sources: Sahih al-Bukhari 6139 (Salman advising Abu Darda: your Lord, your own self, and your family all have a right over you — endorsed directly by the Prophet ﷺ).
