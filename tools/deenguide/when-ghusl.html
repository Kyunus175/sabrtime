I need to understand when ghusl is required

Your Situation

You're not sure whether your current state requires a full ghusl (bath) rather than just wudu.

What to know

Ghusl becomes obligatory (fard) in a specific, limited set of situations — it isn't required for everyday impurity, only for what's called a state of janabah (major ritual impurity) or after menstruation/postpartum bleeding ends.

What you can do

  • Ghusl is required after sexual intercourse, even without ejaculation, according to the majority scholarly position (Sahih Muslim 348).
  • Ghusl is required after ejaculation with desire, whether while awake or during a wet dream.
  • Ghusl is required for women once menstrual or postpartum bleeding has completely stopped.
  • To perform ghusl: make the intention, then wash your entire body with water, making sure it reaches your skin and the roots of your hair.
  • There's no requirement to undo braided hair for ghusl after janabah — pouring water over the head three times is sufficient (based on the hadith of Umm Salamah, Sahih Muslim 330).
Worth knowing: Ghusl can be delayed until shortly before the next prayer if needed — it doesn't have to happen the instant the state occurs, though sleeping in a state of janabah without at least making wudu first is discouraged.
Sources: Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:6 · Sahih Muslim 348–349 (ghusl obligatory upon intercourse) · Sahih Muslim 330 (Umm Salamah, on hair during ghusl)
