I'm traveling during Ramadan — do I have to fast?

Your Situation

You have a journey during Ramadan and aren't sure whether you're required to keep fasting, or if travel itself is a valid reason to break your fast.

What to know

Travel is one of the two exemptions the Quran names directly for fasting — alongside illness — and the choice is explicitly left to you, not decided by how difficult the journey looks from the outside.

What you can do

  • Once you've genuinely started your journey (the same travel distance that applies to shortening prayers), you may choose to fast or not — the Prophet ﷺ told a companion who preferred to keep fasting while traveling, 'Fast if you wish, and do not fast if you wish.'
  • If you break your fast while traveling, make up the missed days later, once you're back and able, before the next Ramadan begins.
  • If fasting is easy for you and doesn't cause real hardship, continuing to fast is fine and doesn't need to be avoided out of caution.
  • If the journey is genuinely difficult (heat, exertion, long travel days), it isn't considered extra pious to insist on fasting when it's truly hard — the Prophet ﷺ himself discouraged pushing through a fast that was clearly causing hardship for people traveling with him.
Worth knowing: The intention to travel isn't enough on its own — the exemption applies once you've actually left your home area and are underway, not simply because you're planning a trip.
Sources: Surah Al-Baqarah 2:184-185 · Sahih al-Bukhari 1943 / Sahih Muslim 1121 (Hamza ibn Amr al-Aslami)
