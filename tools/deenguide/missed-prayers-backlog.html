I have a backlog of many missed prayers

Your Situation

You've missed prayers over an extended period — weeks, months, or years — and aren't sure how to even begin making them up.

What to know

This is treated the same as any missed prayer: the obligation doesn't disappear, but it also isn't a life sentence — there's a practical, structured way to work through even a large backlog.

What you can do

  • Estimate the number as closely as you reasonably can — even an approximate count is accepted; you don't need an exact log.
  • A common, manageable method: add one extra qaza prayer of the same kind after each of your five daily prayers (e.g. pray an extra Dhuhr after today's Dhuhr) — this steadily works through the backlog without overwhelming your day.
  • According to the majority of scholars (Hanafi, Maliki, Hanbali), missed prayers should ideally be made up in the order they were missed — but Hanafi and Maliki scholars specifically waive this ordering requirement once the backlog spans more than a day and a night, since keeping strict order over a long backlog would be too difficult.
  • Start now rather than waiting for a 'complete' count or a perfect system — consistency matters more than precision here.
Worth knowing: There's genuine scholarly difference on how realistic it is to make up every single prayer from a very large, decades-old backlog versus focusing on sincere repentance going forward. If your backlog is very large, it's worth discussing your specific situation with a knowledgeable teacher rather than trying to resolve it alone.
Sources: Sahih Muslim 684 (Anas ibn Malik — the expiation for a missed prayer is to pray it when remembered) · Al-Mughni by Ibn Qudamah (majority view on order; Hanafi/Maliki exception for long backlogs)
