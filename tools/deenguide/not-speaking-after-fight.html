I'm Not Speaking to Someone After a Fight

Your Situation

Something happened between you and someone — a family member, a friend — and now you're not speaking. Days have passed, and neither of you has reached out first.

What to know

The Prophet ﷺ set an explicit limit on exactly this: cutting someone off is only tolerated for up to three days. Beyond that, staying silent stops being a reaction to hurt and becomes something the Prophet ﷺ directly forbade.

What you can do

  • Know the limit by name: "It is not lawful for a Muslim to desert his brother for more than three nights" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6076) — three days is the maximum, not a suggestion.
  • If it's already been longer, the way back is specified too: when you meet, whoever greets first is named as the better of the two (Sahih al-Bukhari 6237) — you don't need the conflict resolved to take that first step, just the greeting.
  • The same hadith (Bukhari 6076) pairs "do not desert one another" directly with "do not hate one another, nor be jealous of one another" — treating a cold silence and festering jealousy as the same family of harm, not separate issues.
  • A text, a call, or just showing up and saying salam all count as greeting first — it doesn't have to be a resolution of whatever caused the rift, just an opening.
This limit is specifically about personal disputes and hurt feelings — it doesn't apply to protecting yourself from someone who is genuinely abusive or unsafe to be around, where distance can be the right and necessary choice.
Sources: Sahih al-Bukhari 6076 (Anas ibn Malik — do not hate or envy one another, and it is unlawful to desert a fellow Muslim for more than three nights) · Sahih al-Bukhari 6237 (Abu Ayyub — the better of the two is the one who greets first).
