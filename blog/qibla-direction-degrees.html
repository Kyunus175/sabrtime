The Short Answer

Qibla direction, expressed precisely, is a compass bearing in degrees from true north — a number between 0° and 360° — pointing toward the Kaaba in Makkah. It's different for every location on Earth. From New York it's about 58.5°. From Mumbai it's about 280°. From London it's about 119°. Knowing your city's approximate degree gets you far more precise than a general "east" or "west" answer.

Why "East or West" Isn't Precise Enough

Cardinal directions like east, west, or northeast are useful shorthand, but they each cover a 90-degree slice of the compass. Two people who are both technically "facing northeast" could still be 40 or 50 degrees apart from each other — enough to matter for accurate prayer alignment. A degree figure removes that ambiguity entirely. If you want the general cardinal-direction breakdown by region first, see our guide on whether Qibla is east or west for your country.

How to Read a Bearing Like "58.5°"

A compass bearing is measured clockwise from true north: 0° is north, 90° is east, 180° is south, and 270° is west. So "58.5°" means: stand facing true north, then rotate just under 60° toward the east — you'll land almost exactly between northeast and east-northeast. Most phone compass apps display this as a number in real time as you turn, which is far more exact than eyeballing "roughly northeast."

How the Degree Is Calculated

Every bearing in the table above comes from the same great-circle formula used by accurate Qibla compasses: given your latitude and longitude and the Kaaba's fixed coordinates, the calculation finds the shortest path across the curved surface of the Earth and expresses its initial direction as a bearing. This is why, for example, New York's Qibla points northeast rather than directly across the Atlantic toward Makkah — the shortest real-world path curves north. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Qibla direction in degrees?
It depends on your location. The Qibla is expressed as a compass bearing in degrees from true north, measured to the Kaaba's coordinates (21.4225°N, 39.8262°E). For example, it's about 58.5° from New York, 267° from Delhi, and 119° from London — every city has its own exact figure.
How many degrees is Qibla from New York?
From New York City, the Qibla bearing is approximately 58.5° from true north, which falls in the northeast (ENE) sector — not east, and not a straight line across the Atlantic.
How many degrees is Qibla from India?
It varies by city. From New Delhi it's about 267° (nearly due west, slightly north), from Mumbai about 280° (west-northwest), and from Kolkata about 278°. The angle shifts several degrees across the country.
What coordinates does Qibla direction calculation use for the Kaaba?
The standard reference point is 21.4225°N, 39.8262°E, the Kaaba's location within the Masjid al-Haram in Makkah, Saudi Arabia. All accurate Qibla bearing calculations are based on the great-circle path from a given location to this exact point.
How accurate does my Qibla bearing need to be?
Being off by a few degrees is not a concern for daily prayer — Islamic scholarship holds that a reasonable, good-faith estimate of the direction is sufficient, especially far from Makkah. A compass reading within 5-10 degrees of the calculated bearing is more than adequate.
Is the Qibla bearing the same everywhere in a country?
No. The exact bearing changes gradually with longitude and latitude, so it can differ by several degrees between cities in the same country. For an exact reading, calculate the bearing from your specific coordinates rather than using a single national figure.

A degree figure won't change how you pray — but it removes the guesswork. Once you know your number, a compass app confirms it in seconds, and you never have to wonder again.
