Durood Tunajjina: Full Text, Meaning, and When to Recite It

What Is Durood Tunajjina?

Durood Tunajjina (also spelled Tunjina or Tanjeena) is a salawat — a supplication sending blessings upon Prophet Muhammad ﷺ — recited especially in times of hardship, fear, or need. The name comes from the Arabic word tunajjina, meaning "you deliver us" or "you save us," which appears in the opening lines of the prayer itself.

It's one of the most widely recited duroods across the Muslim world, particularly in South Asia, where it's often paired with tasbeeh counting during difficult times — travel, illness, financial strain, or simply a heavy heart.

Full Arabic Text, Transliteration, and Meaning

اللَّهُمَّ صَلِّ عَلَى سَيِّدِنَا مُحَمَّدٍ وَعَلَى آلِ سَيِّدِنَا مُحَمَّدٍ صَلَاةً تُنَجِّينَا بِهَا مِنْ جَمِيعِ الْأَهْوَالِ وَالْآفَاتِ، وَتَقْضِي لَنَا بِهَا جَمِيعَ الْحَاجَاتِ، وَتُطَهِّرُنَا بِهَا مِنْ جَمِيعِ السَّيِّئَاتِ، وَتَرْفَعُنَا بِهَا عِنْدَكَ أَعْلَى الدَّرَجَاتِ، وَتُبَلِّغُنَا بِهَا أَقْصَى الْغَايَاتِ مِنْ جَمِيعِ الْخَيْرَاتِ فِي الْحَيَاةِ وَبَعْدَ الْمَمَاتِ، إِنَّهُ سَمِيعٌ قَرِيبٌ مُجِيبُ الدَّعَوَاتِ
Allahumma salli 'ala Sayyidina Muhammadin wa 'ala aali Sayyidina Muhammadin, salaatan tunajjina biha min jami'il ahwaali wal-aafaat, wa taqdi lana biha jami'al-haajaat, wa tutahhiruna biha min jami'is-sayyi'aat, wa tarfa'una biha 'indaka a'lad-darajaat, wa tuballighuna biha aqsal-ghaayaat, min jami'il-khayraati fil-hayaati wa ba'dal-mamaat. Innahu Samee'un Qareebun Mujeebud-da'awaat.
"O Allah, send blessings upon our master Muhammad and upon the family of our master Muhammad — blessings through which You deliver us from all fears and afflictions, fulfil for us all our needs, purify us from all sins, raise us in Your presence to the highest ranks, and bring us to the furthest extent of good in this life and after death. Indeed, You are All-Hearing, Ever-Near, the Answerer of prayers."

The Story Behind the Name

The traditional account, passed down in classical sources such as Imam al-Fakahani's Fajr al-Munir, describes a sailor named Shaykh Musa al-Darir caught in a violent storm at sea. As the ship struggled against the waves, he fell asleep and, in a dream, saw the Prophet ﷺ instructing him to have everyone aboard recite this exact prayer a thousand times. They did — and the storm is said to have subsided.

This account is a traditionally recounted story passed through generations of scholarly commentary, not a verified historical record — it's worth understanding it that way: as part of the durood's heritage and the reason for its name, rather than a documented event.

When It's Recited

Times of hardship or fear. Illness, financial strain, travel, or general anxiety are the most common occasions.
As part of daily adhkar. Many recite it a fixed number of times after prayer, alongside other salawat.
Before a journey. Tied to its origin story, it's often recited before travel, especially by sea.

A Note on Authenticity

Durood Tunajjina is not found in Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim, or the other major hadith collections, and it doesn't carry the same status as Durood Ibrahim — the durood the Prophet ﷺ directly taught his companions and which Muslims recite within the prayer itself (tashahhud). It's a devotional salawat that circulated through later scholarly tradition, including its inclusion in Imam al-Jazuli's well-known collection Dala'il al-Khayrat.

Sending blessings upon the Prophet ﷺ is itself clearly encouraged in the Quran (33:56), and unlike some other duroods, the wording of Tunajjina itself doesn't raise the kind of doctrinal concerns some scholars have flagged elsewhere — it's a straightforward request for Allah's blessings and relief. As with any devotional practice outside the Quran and authentic hadith, it's worth knowing its origin rather than assuming it carries the same weight as a directly transmitted prophetic teaching.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does Tunajjina mean?
Tunajjina comes from the Arabic root meaning "to save" or "to deliver." The name reflects the durood's core request — asking Allah to send blessings on the Prophet ﷺ in a way that delivers the reciter from fear and hardship.
Is Durood Tunajjina from an authentic hadith?
It is not directly narrated in the major hadith collections like Sahih al-Bukhari or Sahih Muslim. It is a devotional salawat that has circulated in Islamic scholarly tradition for centuries, distinct from durood taught directly by the Prophet ﷺ such as Durood Ibrahim.
When do Muslims recite Durood Tunajjina?
It is commonly recited during times of hardship, fear, illness, or before travel, as well as part of regular daily adhkar. There is no single fixed count — some recite it a few times, others in larger repetitions during difficulty.
Can I recite Durood Tunajjina instead of Durood Ibrahim in prayer?
No. Durood Ibrahim is the specific durood taught by the Prophet ﷺ for recitation within the prayer (tashahhud). Durood Tunajjina is a separate devotional supplication recited outside of formal salah, not a replacement within it.

Whatever brings someone to a moment of sending blessings on the Prophet ﷺ — a storm, a hard week, or just a quiet habit after Fajr — the intention behind it tends to matter more than the exact circumstances that first attached a name to the words.
