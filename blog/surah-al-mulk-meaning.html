Surah Al-Mulk: Meaning, Benefits & Why It's Read Before Sleeping

Thirty verses on who truly holds control — and the hadith explaining why it's become part of millions of Muslims' nightly routine.

Overview

Surah Al-Mulk is the 67th chapter of the Quran, made up of 30 verses, located in the 29th juz. It's a Makkan surah, revealed before the Prophet's migration to Madinah, and it belongs to a group of surahs concerned with reminding people of Allah's power and the reality of the Hereafter — themes typical of the Makkan period.

What "Al-Mulk" Means

"Al-Mulk" translates to "The Dominion" or "The Sovereignty." The surah opens by declaring that all control belongs to Allah, and the name is drawn directly from its first verse. It's sometimes referred to by scholars as Al-Waqiyah or Al-Munjiyah — "the protector" or "the saving one" — names that reflect the virtue attributed to it rather than its literal meaning.

Main Themes

The surah moves through a few connected ideas:

  • Allah's sovereignty: everything that exists is under His control, and He alone determines life and death as a test of who does best in deed.
  • The precision of creation: the surah points to the heavens, layered without flaw, as evidence of deliberate design rather than chance.
  • Consequence for denial: it describes the fate awaiting those who reject the truth, and their own admission, when confronted with it, that they failed to listen or reflect.
  • Provision and control: it closes by asking who could provide for people if Allah withheld sustenance — reinforcing total dependence on Him.

The Hadith Behind Its Virtue

The specific virtue attached to Surah Al-Mulk comes from an authentic hadith, reported in at-Tirmidhi and Musnad Ahmad, in which the Prophet ﷺ described a surah of thirty verses that intercedes on behalf of its reciter until they are forgiven — identifying it as "Tabarak alladhi bi-yadihil mulk," the opening words of this surah.

A separate narration links the surah to protection from the punishment of the grave, which is the basis for its strong association with nightly recitation.

Worth knowing: unlike some devotional texts that circulate with unverified claims attached, the core virtue of Surah Al-Mulk — intercession and protection tied to consistent nightly recitation — rests on hadith found in recognized collections, not just popular tradition.

Why It's Recited Before Sleeping

It's reported that the Prophet ﷺ would not go to sleep until he had recited Surah Al-Mulk and Surah As-Sajdah. Over time this became an established part of the nightly Sunnah for many Muslims — read after Isha prayer, right before sleep, either from memory or from a written or app-based reference.

If you're building the habit

Thirty verses can feel long to read from memory at first. Most people start by following along with a transliteration or translation each night, and only gradually reach the point of reciting it fluently — there's no requirement to have it memorized to benefit from reading it.

Selected Verses: Arabic & Meaning

تَبَارَكَ الَّذِي بِيَدِهِ الْمُلْكُ وَهُوَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ

Verse 1: "Blessed is He in whose hand is all sovereignty, and He is over all things competent."

الَّذِي خَلَقَ الْمَوْتَ وَالْحَيَاةَ لِيَبْلُوَكُمْ أَيُّكُمْ أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًا

Verse 2: "He who created death and life to test you as to which of you is best in deed."

الَّذِي خَلَقَ سَبْعَ سَمَاوَاتٍ طِبَاقًا ۖ مَا تَرَىٰ فِي خَلْقِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ مِن تَفَاوُتٍ

Verse 3: "He who created seven heavens in layers. You do not see in the creation of the Most Merciful any inconsistency."

قُلْ هُوَ الَّذِي أَنشَأَكُمْ وَجَعَلَ لَكُمُ السَّمْعَ وَالْأَبْصَارَ وَالْأَفْئِدَةَ ۖ قَلِيلًا مَا تَشْكُرُونَ

Verse 23: "Say, 'It is He who has produced you and made for you hearing and vision and hearts; little are you grateful.'"

The full 30 verses continue this movement — from declaring Allah's power, through the design of creation, to the fate of the deniers, closing with a reminder that all provision comes from Him alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Surah Al-Mulk about?
Surah Al-Mulk (chapter 67, "The Dominion") is about Allah's complete sovereignty over creation. Across its 30 verses it reflects on the design of the heavens, the purpose of life and death as a test, and the fate of those who deny the truth, closing with a reminder that only Allah controls provision and guidance.
What are the benefits of reciting Surah Al-Mulk?
A hadith reported in at-Tirmidhi and Musnad Ahmad describes a 30-verse surah that intercedes for its reciter until they are forgiven, identified as Surah Al-Mulk. Other narrations link it to protection from the punishment of the grave, which is why it's traditionally recited before sleeping.
Why is Surah Al-Mulk read before sleeping?
It's reported that the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ would not sleep before reciting Surah Al-Mulk, and hadith describe it as a protector from the punishment of the grave. This practice has been passed down as part of the Sunnah of the nightly routine.
How many verses does Surah Al-Mulk have?
Surah Al-Mulk has 30 verses. It is the 67th chapter of the Quran and is located in the 29th juz (part).

Whether or not the nightly habit is already part of your routine, Surah Al-Mulk is worth sitting with for its message alone — a short, complete reminder of who actually holds control, right before the day ends.

Sources: Surah Al-Mulk (Quran 67); hadith on its virtue reported in Jami' at-Tirmidhi and Musnad Ahmad.
