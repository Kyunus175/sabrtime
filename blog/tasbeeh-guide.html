How to Do Tasbeeh — A Complete Guide

Tasbeeh is one of the simplest and most rewarding acts of dhikr — a few words, repeated with presence of heart, that keep you connected to Allah throughout the day.

What Tasbeeh actually means

"Tasbeeh" comes from the root s-b-h, meaning to glorify or declare Allah free of any imperfection. In practice, the word is used both for the specific phrase "SubhanAllah" and, more broadly, for the whole practice of counted remembrance — often done with the fingers or a string of beads (a misbaha).

The three core phrases

سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ
SubhanAllah
Glory be to Allah
Recommended: 33x
الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ
Alhamdulillah
All praise is due to Allah
Recommended: 33x
اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ
Allahu Akbar
Allah is the Greatest
Recommended: 34x

Reciting these 33-33-34 after each of the five daily prayers, totaling 100, is a practice taught by the Prophet ﷺ (Sahih Muslim 596) — a simple habit that's easy to carry into daily life.

When to do Tasbeeh

After each prayer

The most well-established time — the 33-33-34 count fits naturally into the few minutes right after salah, before you get up.

Morning and evening adhkar

Many Muslims set aside a few minutes after Fajr and after Asr or Maghrib for a longer session of tasbeeh, often alongside other adhkar.

Idle moments

Commuting, waiting in line, or any moment your hands are free but your mind is idle — these are natural windows to slip in dhikr without needing to set aside dedicated time.

Common mistakes to avoid

Rushing the count. Racing to hit a number turns tasbeeh into a mechanical exercise. Slower, fewer repetitions with real presence of heart outweigh a fast hundred said without thought.
Losing track and guessing. Losing count mid-way and estimating defeats the purpose of a counted practice — this is exactly why a physical or digital counter is genuinely useful, not just a gimmick.
Treating it as a one-time habit. Doing it intensely for a few days after Ramadan and then dropping it entirely is common. Consistency in small, repeatable amounts builds a habit that lasts.
Ignoring the meaning entirely. It's valid to recite even while your Arabic is still developing, but returning to the meaning occasionally — what you're actually declaring about Allah — deepens the practice significantly.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need prayer beads (misbaha) to do Tasbeeh? No — beads are a tool, not a requirement. Many people count on their fingers (a practice the Prophet ﷺ himself did, per Abu Dawud 1502), and a digital counter works just as well.
Can I do Tasbeeh silently, without moving my lips? Scholars differ slightly, but moving the tongue or lips, even quietly, is generally preferred over purely mental recitation, since dhikr is a verbal act of worship.
Is there a wrong time to do Tasbeeh? There is no fixed restriction — dhikr can be done at almost any time, though it's discouraged inside the bathroom or during specific moments of the prayer itself where other words are prescribed instead.
What if I lose count partway through? Simply start the current set again, or round down to the last number you're confident of. This is precisely the problem a tasbeeh counter app removes — no more re-counting from memory.
