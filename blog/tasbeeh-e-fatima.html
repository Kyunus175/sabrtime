Tasbeeh e Fatima: Meaning, Method, and the Story Behind It

Fatima (RA) came to her father exhausted from housework, hoping for a servant to ease the load. What she received instead was something she kept for the rest of her life — and Muslims still recite today.

The story behind Tasbeeh e Fatima

Fatima (RA), the daughter of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, spent her days grinding grain by hand and hauling water — work hard enough to leave marks on her palms. Hearing that some servants had become available, she went to ask her father for help.

She didn't find him at home, so she left her request with his wife. That night, the Prophet ﷺ came to Fatima and her husband Ali (RA) himself. As narrated by Ali (RA), the Prophet ﷺ said: "Shall I not direct you to something better than what you have asked for? When you go to bed, say SubhanAllah thirty-three times, Alhamdulillah thirty-three times, and Allahu Akbar thirty-four times. It is better for you than a servant." (Sahih al-Bukhari 3113, Sahih Muslim 2727)

Fatima (RA) accepted it, and this exact combination has been recited by Muslims ever since — known simply as Tasbeeh e Fatima, or Tasbeeh Fatima Zahra.

The method — 33, 33, 34

سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ
SubhanAllah
Glory be to Allah
33 times
ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّٰهِ
Alhamdulillah
All praise is due to Allah
33 times
اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ
Allahu Akbar
Allah is the Greatest
34 times

That totals 100 repetitions — a complete set that takes most people two to three minutes once memorized. Some recite it phrase by phrase in order; others pause slightly between each repetition to keep the count accurate and the mind present, rather than rushing through it.

When to recite it

Before sleeping. This is the original context from the hadith — the last dhikr before you close your eyes for the night.
After each obligatory prayer. Widely practiced as part of the dhikr that follows Fard salah, alongside other after-prayer remembrances.

A note on variations

The numbers — 33, 33, and 34 — stay consistent across the narrations Muslims rely on. Depending on the source or tradition you follow, you may come across slightly different orderings of the three phrases, or additional narrations that build on the practice. The core of it, though, remains the same gift the Prophet ﷺ gave his daughter: a fixed, memorable dhikr that anyone can carry with them, with nothing needed but a few quiet minutes.

Why it's still recited today

Beyond the historical significance, the practice is simple enough that it doesn't require memorizing anything lengthy, doesn't need a specific setting, and takes only a couple of minutes. That combination — meaningful, short, and repeatable — is likely why it has remained one of the most widely recited forms of dhikr across the Muslim world for over a thousand years.

Why this one matters to me

Tasbeeh e Fatima was one of the first fixed dhikr routines I learned as a kid, and it's still the one I default to when I don't have the mental space to choose something else. That's part of what makes it so effective — it doesn't ask you to decide anything, just to count.

Frequently asked questions

What is Tasbeeh e Fatima? Tasbeeh e Fatima is a dhikr taught by Prophet Muhammad ﷺ to his daughter Fatima (RA) — 33 repetitions of SubhanAllah, 33 of Alhamdulillah, and 34 of Allahu Akbar, recited after prayer or before sleeping.
Why is it called Tasbeeh e Fatima? It is named after Fatima (RA), the Prophet's daughter, because he taught it to her directly when she came to him tired from housework and asked for help.
Is the order of the phrases fixed? The core numbers — 33, 33, and 34 — are consistent across narrations. The exact order in which the three phrases are recited is described slightly differently across various narrations and traditions, though the total of 100 remains the same.
Can I recite Tasbeeh e Fatima using a digital counter? Yes. Keeping an accurate count matters more than the tool used to count it — beads, fingers, or a digital tasbeeh counter all serve the same purpose.

A father teaching his tired daughter something to hold onto, that then outlived them both by over a thousand years — that's the quiet weight behind these hundred words. It costs nothing, needs no special moment, and is available the instant you need it.
