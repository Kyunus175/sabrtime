Why Friday Gets Its Own Checklist

Friday holds a distinct place in the week — described in hadith as the best day the sun rises on. Unlike the five daily prayers, Jumu'ah comes with a cluster of additional Sunnahs specific to this one day, which means it's easy to either forget several of them or do them without knowing which carry the most emphasis. This list is ordered by priority, not just alphabetically or by time of day, so you know what to protect first if Friday gets hectic.

The Full Checklist

☐
Perform ghusl (a full bath) High priority
Recommended before heading to Jumu'ah prayer, tied directly to hadith on the virtue of the day.
☐
Recite Surah Al-Kahf High priority
Any time from Thursday's Maghrib through Friday's Maghrib counts as "on Friday" by most scholarly reckoning.
☐
Go to the mosque early High priority
Reward is described as increasing the earlier you arrive — hadith compares early arrivals to those who sacrifice a camel, then a cow, then a sheep, in descending order as time passes.
☐
Listen to the khutbah in silence High priority
Talking during the khutbah, even to say "be quiet" to someone else, is described in hadith as nullifying the reward of that Jumu'ah.
☐
Send abundant salawat on the Prophet ﷺ Recommended
Friday is specifically highlighted in hadith as a day to increase salawat beyond the usual amount.
☐
Make dua in the last hour before Maghrib Recommended
Described as a time when dua is especially likely to be answered — see the section below for how to identify it.
☐
Wear clean or new clothes Recommended
Dressing well for Jumu'ah is part of honoring the day, distinct from ordinary daily wear.
☐
Apply perfume or fragrance Extra
A smaller Sunnah but explicitly mentioned alongside ghusl and clean clothes in hadith describing the day.
☐
Trim nails / groom Extra
Commonly practiced on Fridays as part of general personal grooming, though not as strongly emphasized as the items above.

The Last Hour Before Maghrib

This is one of the most-asked-about parts of the Friday Sunnahs, and it's genuinely a bit confusing because it isn't a fixed clock time. The most well-known scholarly opinion places this special window of likely-answered dua as the final portion of time between Asr and Maghrib on Friday — meaning it shifts every week and by location, since it's tied to your local Maghrib time, not a universal hour.

Practical way to catch it: check your local Maghrib time for that Friday, then set a reminder for roughly 45–60 minutes before it. Use that window for dua, dhikr, and reflection rather than other tasks.

If You're Genuinely Short on Time

Not every Friday allows for the full list — work, travel, and daily life don't pause. If you have to triage, the four items marked "High priority" above (ghusl, Surah Al-Kahf, early mosque arrival, silent khutbah attendance) carry the strongest emphasis in the hadith literature specifically about this day. The "Recommended" and "Extra" tiers add real reward but are more flexible if the day is genuinely constrained.

Consistency over completeness

A Friday where you hit the four high-priority items every week will likely serve you better long-term than an inconsistent Friday where you occasionally do all nine but often do none. Build the habit on the essentials first, then layer in the rest.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the main Sunnahs of Friday (Jumu'ah)?
The main Friday Sunnahs include performing ghusl (a full bath), wearing clean or new clothes and perfume, reciting Surah Al-Kahf, arriving early to the mosque, listening silently to the khutbah, sending abundant salawat on the Prophet, and making dua in the last hour before Maghrib.
Which Friday Sunnah is most important if I only have time for one?
Arriving early to the mosque and listening attentively and silently to the khutbah carry some of the strongest emphasis in the hadith, alongside reciting Surah Al-Kahf. If time is limited, prioritizing these over less time-sensitive Sunnahs like extra perfume or trimming nails is reasonable.
What time is the "last hour" on Friday for dua?
The most well-known scholarly opinion places it as the final portion of time between Asr and Maghrib on Friday. Since this window shifts with the local Maghrib time each week, it isn't a fixed clock time — check your local Maghrib time and count back roughly an hour.
Is reciting Surah Al-Kahf on Friday obligatory?
No, it's a recommended Sunnah, not an obligation. It's highly encouraged based on hadith describing its virtue on this specific day, but missing it doesn't carry sin — it's simply a missed opportunity for extra reward.
Do these Sunnahs apply to women as well?
Sunnahs like ghusl, Surah Al-Kahf, salawat, and dua in the last hour apply to both men and women. Jumu'ah congregational prayer attendance at the mosque is obligatory for men and not obligatory for women, though women who do attend or pray Dhuhr at home can still observe the other Sunnahs of the day.

Nine Sunnahs, four of them carrying most of the weight — treat this as a weekly rhythm rather than a perfection checklist, and it holds up even on the Fridays that don't go as planned.

Sources: Sahih al-Bukhari 881 (ghusl on Friday) · Sahih al-Bukhari 929 (early arrival and the khutbah) · Sahih al-Bukhari 934 (silence during the khutbah).
