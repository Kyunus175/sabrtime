Dua After Fajr Prayer: What to Recite

A few minutes after Fajr, before the phone comes out, carry more than most people realize. Here's exactly what the Prophet (peace be upon him) is reported to have said and done in that window — each one sourced.

Why the Minutes After Fajr Matter

Fajr is usually the prayer with the least resistance around it — the house is quiet, notifications haven't started, and there's nowhere to rush off to yet. The Prophet (peace be upon him) is reported to have used exactly this window consistently: specific duas said every time, right after the salam, before he spoke to anyone else.

None of what follows is long. The core of it takes under five minutes. What matters more than length is that each one is a specific, transmitted wording — not a general suggestion to "make dua after Fajr," but the Prophet's own words for it.

The Dua for Knowledge, Provision & Accepted Deeds

If you only take one thing from this page, this is it — the dua Umm Salamah reported the Prophet (peace be upon him) saying every single time he finished the dawn prayer, before speaking to anyone.

اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَسْأَلُكَ عِلْمًا نَافِعًا وَرِزْقًا طَيِّبًا وَعَمَلًا مُتَقَبَّلًا
Allahumma inni as'aluka 'ilman nafi'an, wa rizqan tayyiban, wa 'amalan mutaqabbalan
"O Allah, I ask You for beneficial knowledge, goodly provision, and acceptable deeds."

Three requests, in order — knowledge that actually benefits you (not just information), provision that's good rather than merely large, and deeds Allah accepts rather than just deeds performed. It's short enough to say from memory by the second or third morning.

Source: Sunan Ibn Majah 925, graded Sahih.

The Declaration of Tawhid, Ten Times

A second dhikr specifically tied to Fajr and Maghrib — the two prayers that bookend the night.

لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَحْدَهُ لَا شَرِيكَ لَهُ، لَهُ الْمُلْكُ وَلَهُ الْحَمْدُ، يُحْيِي وَيُمِيتُ، وَهُوَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ
La ilaha illallah, wahdahu la sharika lah, lahul-mulku wa lahul-hamd, yuhyi wa yumeet, wa huwa 'ala kulli shay'in qadeer
"There is no god but Allah, alone, without partner. To Him belongs all sovereignty and praise. He gives life and causes death, and He is over all things capable."

Recited ten times after Fajr and again ten times after Maghrib. A closely related version of this same declaration, said one hundred times in a day, is described elsewhere as carrying the reward of freeing ten slaves along with a hundred recorded good deeds — this Fajr/Maghrib-specific version is the shorter, anchored daily habit.

Source: Recorded in Hisn al-Muslim (Fortress of the Muslim) 72, referenced to Jami' at-Tirmidhi and Musnad Ahmad.

The Three Quls for Protection

Surah Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq, and An-Nas — the three short surahs known as "the Quls" — recited three times each.

The hadith: "Recite Surah Al-Ikhlas and Al-Mu'awwidhatayn (Al-Falaq and An-Nas) three times in the morning and the evening. It will suffice you in all respects."

Three short surahs, three times each — under two minutes — covering tawhid (Al-Ikhlas) and protection from harm and whispers (Al-Falaq, An-Nas). This is one of the few adhkar the Prophet (peace be upon him) described with the word "suffice," which is why it's treated as close to non-negotiable in most morning-adhkar routines.

Source: Sunan Abi Dawud 5082, also recorded in Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3575.

The Tasbeeh After Fajr

The same 33-33-34 tasbeeh recited after every obligatory prayer applies here too — SubhanAllah 33 times, Alhamdulillah 33 times, Allahu Akbar 34 times, totaling 100. If you're using a counter, this is usually the easiest one to fold into the routine since it needs no thinking, just counting.

Source: Sahih Muslim 596.

Sitting Until Sunrise, Then Two Rak'ahs

This is the one people usually share as "a full Hajj and Umrah for a few minutes of dhikr" — and it's worth knowing exactly what it says and doesn't say.

"Whoever prays Fajr in congregation, then sits remembering Allah until the sun has risen, then prays two rak'ahs, will have a reward like that of a Hajj and Umrah." He said: "Complete, complete, complete."
Source: Jami' at-Tirmidhi 586.
Worth knowing: Imam at-Tirmidhi himself graded this hadith hasan gharib — good, but narrated through only one chain — and one narrator in it, Abu Zilal, was described by Imam al-Bukhari as muqarib al-hadith (roughly reliable, not top-tier). Scholars have genuinely differed since: some, including Ibn Baz, considered it acceptable to act on given corroborating evidence elsewhere; others graded it weak outright. Either way, the reward described here is specifically for sitting in remembrance until sunrise and then praying two rak'ahs — not a claim that dhikr alone substitutes for Hajj as an obligation.

Practically: if you can stay, even without moving from your spot, remaining in dhikr until sunrise and then praying two rak'ahs is a real, documented practice with real scholarly backing, even where the specific wording of the reward is debated.

Can You Add Your Own Personal Dua Too?

Yes — and many people do, right after finishing the ones above. The transmitted duas and dhikr on this page are Sunnah: specific, tested, and carrying their own stated reward. They aren't a ceiling on what you're allowed to ask for.

Fajr sits right at the edge of the last third of the night, a time already described as one when dua carries particular weight. Once the transmitted adhkar are done, that's a reasonable moment to simply talk to Allah — in Arabic if you have it, in your own language if you don't — about whatever the day ahead actually holds.

A pattern worth noticing: None of these five practices requires more than a few minutes, and none of them requires you to do all five before any of it "counts." The knowledge dua alone, said consistently, is worth more than an ambitious full routine attempted twice and abandoned.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most important dua to say after Fajr?
If you only have a moment, the dua for beneficial knowledge, good provision, and accepted deeds (Sunan Ibn Majah 925) is the one the Prophet (peace be upon him) is reported to have said himself, every time, right after the salam.
Can I make my own personal dua after Fajr, in my own words?
Yes. The specific duas and dhikr in this guide are Sunnah — following the Prophet's own words — but they don't replace personal dua. Many people recite these first, then continue with whatever is actually on their heart, in whatever language they think in.
Do I have to stay until sunrise to get any reward?
No. The specific Hajj-and-Umrah-reward hadith describes sitting in remembrance until sunrise, then praying two rak'ahs — but every dua and dhikr on this page stands on its own. Reciting the knowledge dua or the tasbeeh takes under three minutes and carries its own stated reward regardless of whether you can stay longer.
Is the Hajj and Umrah reward hadith authentic?
Scholars differ. Imam at-Tirmidhi himself graded it hasan gharib (a good but singularly-narrated hadith), and one narrator in its chain, Abu Zilal, was described by Imam al-Bukhari as muqarib al-hadith (roughly reliable, not top-tier). Many later scholars, including Ibn Baz, still considered it acceptable to act on given supporting evidence, while others graded it weak. It's included here with that context rather than as flat, unqualified fact.
Should I recite these in Arabic or is English okay?
The Arabic is the actual wording transmitted from the Prophet (peace be upon him), so it's worth learning over time. If you're not there yet, reciting the transliteration or even just understanding the meaning in English while you memorize is a normal, accepted starting point — not a lesser version of the dua.

None of this needs to happen all at once, or perfectly, from day one. Start with the knowledge dua — it takes fifteen seconds — and let the rest build from there.

Sources: Sunan Ibn Majah 925 · Hisn al-Muslim 72 · Sunan Abi Dawud 5082 · Sahih Muslim 596 · Jami' at-Tirmidhi 586
