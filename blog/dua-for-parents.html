Dua for Parents: For Health, Forgiveness & Long Life

Two duas straight from the Quran — one for mercy on living parents, one for forgiveness for both living and deceased — in Arabic, transliteration, and meaning.

Why This Dua Matters

The Quran doesn't just encourage kindness to parents in general terms — it gives specific words to say for them. Two verses in particular have become the standard duas Muslims recite for their parents: one asking Allah for mercy on them the way they showed mercy raising you, and one asking for forgiveness for both parents and yourself together.

Dua for Mercy on Living Parents

رَّبِّ ارْحَمْهُمَا كَمَا رَبَّيَانِي صَغِيرًا

Transliteration: Rabbi irhamhuma kama rabbayani sagheera

Meaning: "My Lord, have mercy upon them as they brought me up when I was small."

This is from Surah Al-Isra (17:24), part of a passage instructing believers to speak to their parents with gentleness and lower themselves to them "out of mercy," closing with this exact dua. It's short enough to say after every prayer, and directly ties your own mercy back to the mercy they showed raising you.

Dua for Forgiveness (Living or Deceased)

رَبَّنَا اغْفِرْ لِي وَلِوَالِدَيَّ وَلِلْمُؤْمِنِينَ يَوْمَ يَقُومُ الْحِسَابُ

Transliteration: Rabbana-ghfir li wa liwalidayya wa lil-mu'minina yawma yaqumul-hisab

Meaning: "Our Lord, forgive me and my parents and the believers on the Day the account is established."

This is the dua of Prophet Ibrahim (peace be upon him), recorded in Surah Ibrahim (14:41). What makes it distinct from the first dua is scope — it asks forgiveness for yourself, both parents, and the wider community of believers in one line, and it's suitable whether your parents are living or have passed away.

Both together: many people recite the mercy dua (17:24) as a standalone, and add the forgiveness dua (14:41) separately — there's no requirement to combine them, but reciting both covers mercy in this life and forgiveness for the next.

Can I Make Dua for Deceased Parents?

Yes — this is one of the few ways a child's good deeds can continue to reach a parent after death. Alongside dua, ongoing charity given in their name and good character passed down through their upbringing are all understood as ways their reward can continue. The forgiveness dua from Surah Ibrahim works for this directly, since its wording doesn't specify living or deceased.

A simple habit

These two duas are short enough to memorize in one sitting. Many people attach them to something they already do daily — right after finishing a prayer, or right after ending a phone call with a parent — so the habit builds without needing a separate reminder.

When to Recite These Duas

There's no obligatory time. The most common practice is reciting them after the five daily prayers, but they're equally valid said any time — while thinking of your parents, right after speaking with them, or as part of a personal dua list. Consistency matters more than timing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Quranic dua for parents?
The Quran teaches, in Surah Al-Isra 17:24, to say: "Rabbi irhamhuma kama rabbayani sagheera" — "My Lord, have mercy upon them as they brought me up when I was small." It's one of the most widely recited duas for living parents.
What dua can I make for my parents' forgiveness?
Surah Ibrahim 14:41 records the Prophet Ibrahim's dua: "Rabbana-ghfir li wa liwalidayya wa lil-mu'minina yawma yaqumul-hisab" — "Our Lord, forgive me and my parents and the believers on the Day the account is established." It's used for both living and deceased parents.
Can I make dua for deceased parents?
Yes. Praying for deceased parents' forgiveness is encouraged and is considered one of the ways their reward can continue after death, alongside charity given on their behalf and good deeds passed on through their upbringing.
When should I recite dua for my parents?
There's no fixed time. Common practice is reciting it after the five daily prayers, but it can be said any time — while thinking of them, after calling them, or as part of a personal dua routine.

Two short verses, easy to carry with you, and easy to say daily — a small habit that keeps your parents present in your prayers, whether they're a phone call away or already in Allah's mercy.

Sources: Surah Al-Isra 17:24; Surah Ibrahim 14:41.
