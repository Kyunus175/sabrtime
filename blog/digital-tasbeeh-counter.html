What Is a Digital Tasbeeh Counter?

A digital tasbeeh counter is a tool — usually an app — that replaces physical beads for counting repetitions of dhikr like SubhanAllah, Alhamdulillah, and Allahu Akbar. Instead of moving a bead with each phrase, you tap a button or the screen, and the count increases by one. Most also reset automatically at common targets like 33, 99, or 100, so your mind can stay on the dhikr instead of the number. (For the meaning and correct count of each phrase, see our full Tasbeeh guide.)

How It Works

The mechanics are simple by design: open the counter, tap once per repetition, watch the number climb. Some apps go further and track your dhikr history over time — how many days in a row you've completed your after-prayer tasbeeh, for example.

Neither option is "better" in an absolute sense — the counting method has always been a matter of convenience, not religious requirement (see the FAQ below). The real question is which tool actually gets used consistently, and for many people that ends up being whichever one is already in their hand.

Who Digital Tasbeeh Is Best For

Always have your phone anyway? No extra item to remember or carry.
Frequent traveler? One less physical object to pack or misplace.
Trying to build consistency? Seeing your dhikr habit over days and weeks — not just today's count — tends to reinforce the habit itself. If the habit itself hasn't taken hold yet, see our guide on building a daily dhikr habit that actually sticks — a counter helps sustain the habit, it isn't usually what creates it.

How to Choose a Good App

A few things worth checking before you settle on one:

  • No ads interrupting your dhikr — a banner popping up mid-count defeats the purpose.
  • Works without constant internet — dhikr shouldn't depend on signal strength.
  • Simple, distraction-free interface — fewer taps to start counting, not more menus.
  • Free to use — this is a basic act of worship; it shouldn't sit behind a paywall.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a digital tasbeeh counter allowed in Islam?
Yes — the counting tool is a matter of ease, not requirement. Fingers, beads, or a digital counter are all valid; what matters is sincerity and presence of heart during the dhikr itself.
Does a digital counter reset automatically?
Good ones do — most digital tasbeeh counters, including SabrTime's, let you set a target like 33 or 100 and reset automatically once you reach it, so you don't have to watch the number yourself.
What's the difference between tasbeeh and Salatul Tasbeeh?
Tasbeeh broadly means glorifying Allah through repeated phrases like SubhanAllah. Salatul Tasbeeh is a specific four-rakah voluntary prayer with a set structure of these phrases built into it.
Can I use my phone for dhikr instead of beads?
Yes — a phone-based tasbeeh counter works the same way beads do, one tap per repetition. It's especially useful while traveling or when physical beads aren't on hand.

The tool matters far less than the habit it supports. Whether it's beads passed down for generations or a counter on your phone, the goal is the same one that runs through everything at the heart of sabr: showing up for the small act, consistently, day after day.
